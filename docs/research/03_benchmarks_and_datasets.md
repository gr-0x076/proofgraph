# Benchmarks, Datasets & Empirical Evaluation Standards

This document catalogs the primary datasets, evaluation metrics, and benchmarks used in automated fact-checking research, detailing their exact task definitions, dataset distributions, strengths, and blind spots.

  - Dev/Test Labels: Exactly balanced (6,666 per class).
* **Evaluation Metric:** **Strict FEVER Score** — A prediction is counted correct if and only if the veracity label matches AND the retrieved sentences contain at least one complete gold evidence set (max 5 sentences).
* **Blind Spots & Limitations:**
  - **Lexical Artifacts:** Claims were generated by crowdworkers mutating Wikipedia sentences. Negation words ("never", "no", "failed") strongly correlate with . Claim-only models achieve ~62% accuracy without viewing evidence!
  - **Sanitized Corpus:** Evaluates solely against static Wikipedia text. No messy HTML, paywalls, or conflicting live news.
  - **No Disagreement:** Assumes Wikipedia is a monolithic ground truth.

* **2025 Shared Task Innovation (Ev2R Metric):**
  - Replaced legacy METEOR scoring with **Ev2R (Evaluating Evidence Retrieval)** using a 70B parameter LLM judge.
  - A verdict is credited **if and only if** the label is correct AND the Ev2R recall between submitted evidence and gold evidence is $\ge 0.44$.

## 3. SciFact (Scientific Claim Verification)
* **Reference:** Wadden et al., EMNLP 2020 (https://github.com/allenai/scifact)
* **Task Definition:** Given an expert-written biomedical claim and a corpus of 5,183 peer-reviewed research abstracts, retrieve relevant abstracts, extract minimal rationale sentences, and predict  or .
* **Scale:** 1,409 scientific claims (Train: 809; Dev: 300; Test: 300).
* **Key Design Choice:** SciFact **does not assign global claim labels**. It labels **claim-document pairs**, accurately modeling the reality of scientific disagreements across independent clinical studies.
* **Limitations:** Excludes partial support and full-text methodological caveats due to low human inter-annotator agreement.

## 4. CONFACT (Conflicting Evidence for Fact-Checking)
* **Reference:** Ge et al., IJCAI / arXiv:2505.17762 (https://github.com/zoeyyes/CONFACT)
* **Task Definition:** Evaluates RAG models on claims with genuine conflicting evidence retrieved from sources of varying credibility.
* **Empirical Findings:**
  - **Human Misjudgment:** Lay human annotators rated 95.8% of mainstream news as trustworthy, but Media Bias/Fact Check (MBFC) audits revealed that **30% were misleading**. Annotators classified 69.8% of misleading sources as credible!
  - **Vanilla RAG Breakdown:** Standard RAG fails under conflicting evidence because majority-voting heuristics succumb to frequency bias (3 conspiracy blogs outnumber 1 authoritative debunk).
  - **Credibility Augmentation:** Filtering sources at retrieval stage *hurts* accuracy by removing necessary debunking context. But appending source credibility metadata at the **generation stage ({\text{CoT}}$)** yields an absolute **+10% F1 improvement** on LLaMA-3.1-8B.

## 5. The Decomposition Dilemma (arXiv:2411.02400)
* **Reference:** Hu et al., Nov 2024 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02400)
* **Mathematical Trade-Off:**
  284335\Delta A_{\text{err}} = \underbrace{(A(k_d) - A(k_o))}_{\text{Ideal Accuracy Gain } \Delta A} - \underbrace{(E_d + E_r)}_{\text{Cumulative Noise } E}284335
* **When Decomposition Helps:**
  - On long-form, multi-sentence paragraphs where isolating atomic propositions simplifies reasoning for weaker verifiers.
* **When Decomposition Hurts:**
  - On concise or single-sentence claims, decomposing statements introduces context loss, referential ambiguity, and error compounding that lowers overall veracity F1 on frontier models (GPT-4, MiniCheck).
* **Engineering Invariant:**
  - Never decompose single-sentence or concise claims.
  - For complex paragraphs, constrain {\text{sub-claims}} \le N_{\text{sentences}}$.
  - Always run decontextualization to resolve pronouns and entities before querying search backends.
